A Step for Nature: An Eco-Enzyme Activity at the Cisadane River

Background

Environmental pollution has become one of the major issues affecting rivers in Indonesia. The Cisadane River, one of the important rivers in West Java and Banten, plays a significant role in supporting regional ecosystems—around 20 percent of Jakarta’s raw water supply comes from this river. However, rapid urbanization, inadequate waste management, and household wastewater have increasingly threatened the river’s cleanliness. River pollution not only causes damage to ecosystems but can also directly impact human health through diseases such as diarrhea, cholera, and hepatitis if the water contains bacteria, viruses, or parasites. Socially, when clean water becomes scarce, communities may compete for it, leading to conflict.

Recognizing the importance of preserving the environment, Unity in Action has participated in activities that promote sustainability and environmental awareness. One such activity involved a group of students focused on contributing positively to the Cisadane River through the use of eco enzymes. By taking part in this activity, students were allowed to engage directly in environmental conservation rather than simply learn about it in a classroom.

What are Eco Enzymes?

Eco enzymes are multipurpose liquids produced through the fermentation of organic waste. These are usually made from fruit peels, vegetable scraps, brown sugar, and water. The fermentation process may take several months. 60% of the total waste produced in Indonesia is organic waste, yet only 7.5% of it is processed. Organic waste that is piled up in a landfill produces harmful gases to the environment. With eco enzymes, these wastes can be put to good use. They are considered environmentally friendly because they are made from biodegradable materials, while also reducing food waste by repurposing organic matter.

Eco enzymes are commonly used as natural cleaning agents, fertilizers for plants, and solutions that may help improve water quality. They offer a sustainable alternative to chemical-based cleaning products and encourage waste reduction. In this case, eco enzymes are often introduced in rivers to promote healthier environments. Eco enzymes can demonstrate how materials that would normally be thrown away can be transformed into something beneficial.

Activity

The activity began in the afternoon when participating students gathered together in the Columbus Lobby. Students felt curious yet eager to take part in an environmental initiative. Teachers briefly organized and ensured all necessary materials were ready to use. Everyone started moving and arranging the gallons of eco enzymes that would later be poured into the river. After everything was prepared, students boarded a large vehicle and traveled together to the Cisadane River.

The journey took about 30 minutes, with students chatting with one another while waiting to arrive at the destination. Upon finally arriving at a bridge above the Cisadane River, the gallons of eco enzymes were unloaded from the vehicle, while students took each of the containers, opened them, and poured the eco enzymes into the river. Working together, students carefully poured the eco enzymes into the flowing river below. Throughout the activity, many other students were focusing on documenting the moment.

This activity became a valuable learning experience for everyone present. Students were able to be directly involved in the conservation of the environment, experiencing it up close. It also strengthened teamwork and responsibility, as the students worked together with others for one goal. Standing above the river while participating in the process made many students reflect on the importance of environmental preservation and how collective efforts can make a difference, especially after seeing the large amount of garbage piled along the riverbanks, which was highly visible to everyone passing by. Not only that, the river water was cloudy and muddy-brown.

Environmental Impact

Eco enzymes are believed to improve river water quality by acting as a natural catalyst. Some studies and environmental communities suggest that fermented materials can accelerate the decomposition of organic waste, neutralize pollutants, and reduce water cloudiness. This can happen because enzymes speed up the breakdown of harmful organic matter in the water, while also producing disinfecting agents that eliminate foul smells. Furthermore, eco enzymes convert carbon dioxide into carbonate, which may contribute to clearer water conditions. For this reason, eco enzymes are believed to contribute positively to ecosystems due to their organic composition and environmentally friendly nature.

Overall, participating in the eco enzyme activity at the Cisadane River was a meaningful experience. It reminded students that even simple actions can create a bigger impact by raising environmental awareness and encouraging habits of caring for nature. We hope that similar activities can continue in the future and inspire more young people to take part in preserving Indonesia’s environment for many generations to come.
