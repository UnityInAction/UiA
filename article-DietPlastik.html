Diet Plastik Program: The Start of a Plastic-Free Environment

Plastic accumulation in public areas such as schools, offices, and public malls has been an ongoing problem. It poses significant threats to our environment, including landfill overcapacity, marine pollution, and severely impacts human health. Indonesia is one of the world’s largest contributors to global marine plastic pollution. The country generates 7.8 million tons of plastic waste annually, with only a small fraction of that amount being recycled. The rest sits in landfills such as TPST Bantargebang, Jakarta’s main waste processing site, while some may eventually reach the ocean.

Plastic and microplastics may also affect human health, as it contains various chemicals that enhance colour, strength, and durability. Plastic can enter the human body through food, cosmetics, and household products. Moreover, high levels of microplastics in the human body could cause cell damage, cell death, and allergic reactions, which can heighten the risk of developing chronic health conditions. Overall, this can contribute to a weakened immune system and other health concerns. Therefore, plastic waste is a serious issue that affects many people, not just the environment.

Therefore, Unity in Action has conducted the “Diet Plastik” program, aiming at ensuring less plastic usage in Santa Laurensia Senior High School. The program is compulsory for senior high school students, while other students and staff members are encouraged to adopt similar practices in their respective communities. Unity in Action believes that small actions can make a difference in the future. By starting small, the program may provide a meaningful impact in the future.

The “Diet Plastik” program is divided into two main sub-programs, both occurring once a week over the course of several months. The first one requires students to bring their own cutlery and lunch boxes to minimize the amount of plastic waste, especially when purchasing food from the canteen. To encourage students, the Unity in Action team also created a stamp card system. Every week, students who can show their “plastic-free” lunch are rewarded with a stamp. The first few students who complete their stamp cards are rewarded with a voucher that can be spent on canteen food or drinks of their choice. However, students must present their completed card before redeeming the voucher. Through this incentive program, students can experience a hands-on approach to reducing the amount of plastic waste within the school.

The second part of the program requires each class to collect HDPE plastic. HDPE, or High-Density Polyethylene, is a durable, recyclable thermoplastic made from petroleum and is widely used for packaging, piping, and construction materials. In this program, each class must collect HDPE plastic products, such as milk jugs, shampoo bottles, and even bottle caps, every week. At the end of the week, the Unity in Action team gathers the collected HDPE plastic products from each class and weighs them.

In addition, each class must meet specific judging criteria when submitting their HDPE plastics, such as cleanliness and consistency, which contribute to their overall score. The class that collects the largest amount of HDPE plastic products and achieves the highest score is the winner of the competition.

The “Diet Plastik” program isn’t merely a school program; it’s a stepping stone towards raising awareness about plastic usage among the younger generation. As we acknowledge the dangers of plastic pollution, let’s also recognize the significance of programs that help alleviate this issue, such as the “Diet Plastik” program. In return, it’s shaping a more sustainable environment and creating a better future for all.
