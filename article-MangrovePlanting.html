Planting Trees, Saving Lives

Introduction

Indonesia, a country surrounded by 2 major oceans, the Indian and Pacific Oceans, and the South China Sea. Being surrounded by these oceans gives Indonesia coastal islands with extraordinary views and clear water. Indonesia is known for its beautiful beaches, especially in Bali, the Nusa Islands, and Lombok. But despite that, is Indonesia truly known for its idyllic islands or a country that experiences frequent flooding?

Background

Aqueduct Global Flood Analyzer reported that Indonesia is in sixth place among countries, with the most people experiencing floods. Approximately 640,000 individuals are exposed every year. The Indonesian National Agency for Disaster Management (BNPB) also showed how floods occur as often as 464 times every year. Thus, it is concluded that flooding is one of the most common natural disasters in Indonesia, followed by landslides. According to WRI Indonesia, the two main causes of flooding are linked to weather conditions and trees.

  • Trees

    Trees are important as they play a crucial role in keeping the hydrological balance of the drainage basin and enabling the soil to absorb water to prevent flooding. With trees’ presence, natural materials keep the soil absorbent. On the other hand, trees’ roots assist in absorbing minerals and water from the ground.

    Therefore, cutting down trees and replacing them with infrastructure will cause alterations to the hydrologic balance. With fewer trees and ground to absorb rainfall, water will continue to stay on the surface of the ground and increase the chances of flooding.

  • Weather Conditions

    Weather also takes part in contributing to flooding, as it depends on the severity of rainfall. It is considered high-intensity rainfall if it is more than 100 mm of rain each day. This type of rainfall actively participates in causing flooding. According to WRI Indonesia, in Indonesia, it typically experiences rainfall of 248.5 mm, 110 - 197 mm, and 182 - 289 mm. These rainfall data all show being more than 100 mm and can be considered severe. These data were collected in Jayapura Regency in Papua, South Sulawesi, and Bengkulu. In these regions, flood occurrence is quite high.

    This high rainfall in South Sulawesi is caused by a natural phenomenon called the Madden-Julian Oscillation, a strong gust of wind, cloud, and rainfall that circulates the equator, which increases moist air and causes this rainfall. On the other hand, a growing number of clouds are caused by low air pressure in North Papua, causing intense rainfall.

Mangrove’s importance

Climate Change Adaptation reported on June 18, 2025, that Indonesia is challenged to face coastal flood risks caused by climate change. This prediction projects flood risk to increase by 37 percent by the year 2030. As a result, sea levels can rise by around 10 centimeters within this period of time. Not only can increasing sea levels be caused, but extreme weather could also take place on a daily basis, including cyclones, which rarely occur here in Indonesia. Due to these projections, planting mangroves is highly encouraged to help small islands such as Labuan Bajo, located in the East Nusa Tenggara Province.

Mangroves are incredibly needed in Indonesia as they protect the coastline. Mangroves can help reduce storm waves, flooding, wind speed, tsunamis, and erosion. Simultaneously, they can also support a diverse biodiversity by providing habitats for creatures like fish and birds.

Mangroves are able to prevent flooding as they primarily act as a natural buffer. Their roots are dense, tough, and rooted in the ground. Thus, they are able to absorb waves’ strength to prevent flooding. They also absorb rainwater and help balance the hydrological balance, keeping the land from overflowing with rainwater.

The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) stated that mangroves are efficient in withdrawing carbon dioxide and storing carbon, which also aids to prevent climate change. Mangrove roots can also filter impurities, contaminants, and trap debris, which enhances the water quality.

Actions Done and Can be Done

Here at Unity in Action (UIA), we have planted over 1300 Mangroves at Teluknaga alongside external volunteers on June 12, 2024. Funds were raised through donations and later on used to purchase 1000 mangrove seeds. 300 seeds were financed by volunteers. Each student was given 1 to 4 seeds to plant, and each seedling was planted in either a shallow or a deeper area.

In order to prevent Indonesia from flooding frequently, drainage systems must be routinely upgraded to effectively drain rainwater and prevent them from overflowing on the land. Decreasing the frequency of infrastructure and cutting down trees can also help trees and soils absorb the rainfall and prevent runoff. Lastly, planting mangroves is highly suggested, especially in areas that are prone to extreme weather.

Let us together conserve Indonesia and maintain the beauty of the coastline.
